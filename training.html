Lead Yourself

LEAD YOURSELF · GUIDE
Grow as a Leader
Your Personal Growth Plan
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Growth is intentional — Nobody grows by accident; decide to grow before you decide what to do.
22Know yourself first — Use self-assessment (strengths, personality, feedback) to see your real starting point.
33Pick your growth areas — Choose max. two skills or character areas per season instead of improving everything at once.
44Build a simple plan — 30 minutes of deliberate growth daily beats a yearly seminar: reading, podcast, practice, reflection.
55Find growth partners — Share your plan with a mentor or peer who asks you about it regularly.
66Review every quarter — Look back, celebrate progress, adjust the plan — growth compounds when reviewed.
LEAD YOURSELF · GUIDE
Character & Integrity
Trust Is Built Here
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Character is the foundation — People follow who you are before they follow what you say; trust is built or broken here.
22Do what you say — Keep small promises relentlessly; reliability in small things earns authority in big things.
33Be the same person everywhere — On campus, in the village, at church, online: one face, not two.
44Own your mistakes — Admitting errors quickly builds more trust than never making any.
55Handle money &amp; resources cleanly — Transparency in finances and materials protects you and the ministry.
66Guard your private life — Integrity at home sustains integrity at work; hidden compromise always surfaces.
LEAD YOURSELF · GUIDE
Managing Your Time & Priorities
First Things First
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Not everything urgent is important — Learn to sort by impact, not by loudness.
22Plan the week, not just the day — 30 minutes on Monday planning saves hours of firefighting.
33The Jar of Life — Big rocks first: schedule your most important work before the small stuff fills the week.
44Protect focus time — Block 2 hours without Telegram for the work only you can do.
55Learn to say no well — Every yes is a no to something else; declining kindly is a leadership skill.
66Delegate before you drown — If your list never ends, the problem is usually keeping, not workload.
LEAD YOURSELF · GUIDE
Self-Care, Boundaries & Spiritual Health
Lead From a Full Cup
1 DocumentDownload Document
11You can't pour from an empty cup — Sustainable leadership starts with rest, sleep and Sabbath.
22Set working boundaries — Define your hours and response times, and let the team know them.
33Stay spiritually connected — Personal devotion is the engine, not the decoration, of ministry leadership.
44Watch your warning signs — Irritability, cynicism and exhaustion are data, not weakness.
55Have safe people — Every leader needs one place to be completely honest.
66Ask for help early — Talking to your leader or HR before breaking point is strength.

DEVELOP PEOPLE · GUIDE
One-on-One Meetings That Work
Thirty Minutes of Full Attention
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Why one-on-ones matter — 30 minutes of full attention builds more trust than a month of hallway talk.
22A steady rhythm — Fixed frequency (e.g., bi-weekly), fixed length, protected in the calendar — cancelling communicates priorities.
33Their agenda, not a status update — The one-on-one is for the person behind the work — their questions, blockers, growth and wellbeing.
44Four questions that always work — How are you? What's going well? Where are you stuck? How can I help?
55Listen more than you talk — They should talk 60–80% of the time; questions develop people, answers only inform them.
66Close with commitments — End with one or two concrete follow-ups and revisit them next time.
DEVELOP PEOPLE · GUIDE
Delegating Well
Multiply Yourself Through Others
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Why leaders don't delegate — Fear of quality, speed and losing control keeps you small; delegation is how both of you grow.
22Choose the right task &amp; person — Match responsibility to their level: delegate outcomes to strong people, steps to learners.
33Hand over the why, not just the what — People own tasks when they understand purpose and boundaries.
44Agree on check-ins, don't hover — Set milestones together instead of micromanaging in between.
55Let them do it differently — 80% done their way beats 100% done your way — that's the price of growth.
66Stay accountable, then give credit — Delegating the task never delegates your responsibility: stay reachable, debrief, and give credit publicly.
DEVELOP PEOPLE · GUIDE
Coaching Your Team Members
Questions That Grow People
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Coaching ≠ telling — A coach helps people find their own answer; advice creates dependence, questions create growth.
22The GROW path — Goal, Reality, Options, Will: four steps that structure any coaching conversation.
33Ask powerful questions — Open questions (&quot;What have you tried?&quot;) unlock more than suggestions.
44Listen for the real issue — The presented problem is often not the actual problem; stay curious.
55Let them commit — A self-chosen next step has ten times the follow-through of an assigned one.
66Follow up faithfully — Coaching works through rhythm, not through one good conversation.
DEVELOP PEOPLE · GUIDE
Spotting & Developing Future Leaders
Building the Next Generation
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Look for faithfulness, not flash — Potential shows in reliability, hunger to learn and care for others.
22Give responsibility early — Small real ownership (an event, a shift, a project) reveals and builds leaders.
33The apprenticeship path — I do–you watch → we do together → you do–I support → you do–you teach.
44Develop in public — Let emerging leaders run meetings and make visible decisions while you back them.
55Talk about their future — Name what you see in them; many Khmer staff wait for permission to aspire.
66Release, don't hoard — Success is when your best person outgrows your team.
DEVELOP PEOPLE · GUIDE
Performance Conversations (APR)
Honest Reviews Without Surprises
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Feedback is weekly, the review is a summary — The APR only collects what regular one-on-ones already made clear — nothing should surprise.
22Prepare with facts — Collect concrete examples for strengths and growth areas; vagueness helps no one.
33Start with self-assessment — Let them evaluate themselves first — most people are more honest than you expect.
44Balance honor and honesty — Real appreciation and clear growth points in the same conversation.
55Agree development goals — Two or three goals with support attached, written down together.
66Follow the ICF APR process — Use the official forms and timeline so HR and payroll stay aligned.
DEVELOP PEOPLE · GUIDE
Recognition & Keeping Motivation High
Fuel for the Long Run
1 DocumentDownload Document
11What gets recognized gets repeated — Praise is steering, not decoration.
22Be specific, timely and frequent — &quot;Thank you for staying late to fix the pump on Tuesday&quot; beats &quot;good job&quot;; recognized people are far more engaged.
33See the unseen roles — Practical teams (cleaning, security, kitchen, garden) feel overlooked most — your recognition matters double there.
44Know each person's fuel — Public praise, private words, responsibility, learning — motivation is individual.
55Celebrate progress, not only results — Effort and improvement deserve visibility, especially in hard seasons.
66Watch for demotivation signals — Silence, absence and minimum-effort are messages; address them early.
77Connect work to the vision — Every role serves the bigger ICF story — say it often.

COMMUNICATE · GUIDE
Running Great Team Meetings
Meetings Worth Everyone's Time
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Every meeting needs a why — No purpose, no meeting; respect for time is respect for people.
22Information needs no meeting — Pure updates go out in writing; meet only to discuss, decide or connect.
33Prepare a simple agenda — Three points shared beforehand beat ninety unprepared minutes; attention drops after ~20 minutes.
44Start strong, start on time — An opener, a win or a devotion sets energy; starting late trains lateness.
55Involve everyone — Ask the quiet ones, park the long talkers kindly, use pairs for discussion.
66End with decisions &amp; owners — Every point closes with what, who, until when — written down.
77Follow up visibly — Send the action list and start the next meeting by reviewing it.
COMMUNICATE · GUIDE
Giving Feedback
Truth That Builds People
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Feedback is care, not attack — Withholding honest feedback is withholding growth.
22Right time, right place — Praise publicly, correct privately, and never in anger.
33Describe, don't judge — Say what you observed and its effect, not what kind of person they are (SBI: Situation–Behavior–Impact).
44One thing at a time — A single clear point lands; a list of five overwhelms and hardens.
55Invite their view — Feedback is a dialogue: &quot;How do you see it?&quot; often changes the picture.
66End forward-looking — Agree the next step and express confidence — feedback should lift, not flatten.
COMMUNICATE · GUIDE
Receiving Feedback Well
Stay Open, Keep Growing
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Your reaction sets the culture — Leaders who get defensive stop hearing truth forever.
22Listen fully before responding — Breathe, take notes, ask clarifying questions — don't build your defense while they talk.
33Thank first, evaluate later — Gratitude for courage, then 24 hours before deciding what to do with it.
44Look for the grain of truth — Even unfair feedback usually carries 10% you need.
55Ask for feedback actively — &quot;What is one thing I could do better?&quot; — regularly, and mean it.
66Close the loop — Tell people what you changed because of their feedback; nothing builds trust faster.
COMMUNICATE · GUIDE
Difficult Conversations
Say It Early, Say It Kindly
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Don't wait — the price rises — Avoided conversations become conflicts; address issues while they are small.
22Prepare heart and facts — Check your motive (help, not win) and gather concrete observations.
33Open honestly and kindly — Name the issue directly in the first two sentences; small talk fools no one.
44Listen for their story — There is always a perspective you don't have; curiosity de-escalates.
55Search for the way forward together — Move from who's right to what's next.
66Agree, document, follow up — Clear agreements, a short written note, and a check-in date.
COMMUNICATE · GUIDE
Resolving Conflict in Your Team
From Tension to Trust
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Conflict is normal — escalation is optional — Healthy teams disagree; unhealthy teams go silent or explode.
22Go direct first — Encourage the two people to talk directly before you step in (Matthew 18 principle).
33Mediate fairly — Hear both sides separately, then together; no verdicts based on one story.
44Mind culture and face — In the Khmer context, saving face matters: private, calm, respectful settings work.
55Address gossip immediately — Gossip is conflict's fuel; redirect it to the person concerned every time.
66Rebuild the relationship — After resolution, create small cooperation moments so trust regrows.
COMMUNICATE · GUIDE
Everyday Communication
Clear, Kind, Every Day
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Choose the right channel — Complex or emotional topics: face to face; logistics: Telegram; decisions: written.
22Respect response-time rules — Follow the ICF communication guidelines for availability and replies.
33Write clearly and kindly — Short sentences, clear ask, deadline; tone travels badly in text.
44Pass information down faithfully — What you hear in leaders' meetings must reach your team accurately and timely.
55Protect confidentiality — Know what is yours to share and what is not.
66Over-communicate change — People need to hear important news several times, in several ways.

LEAD THE TEAM · GUIDE
Living the ICF Values in Daily Work
Culture Starts With You
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Values are decisions, not posters — Culture is what you tolerate and what you celebrate.
22Model it first — Your team will copy your honesty, punctuality and respect — or your shortcuts.
33Tell value stories — Catch someone living a value and tell the story in the next meeting.
44Hire and onboard by values — Skills can be trained; values must be shared from day one.
55Confront value breaches — One ignored violation teaches everyone the values are optional.
66Review as a team — Once a season, ask honestly: which value are we living, which are we losing?
LEAD THE TEAM · GUIDE
Onboarding New Team Members
Belonging From Day One
1 DocumentDownload Document
11The first week decides — People decide in days whether they belong; plan the first week like an event.
22Prepare before day one — Workplace, tools, schedule and a welcome — nothing says &quot;unimportant&quot; like an unprepared arrival.
33Assign a buddy — A peer for daily questions beats a leader for occasional ones.
44Explain the why of ICF — Vision, story and values before forms and procedures.
55Train in steps with check-ins — Small portions, early practice, weekly reviews in month one.
66Check in at day 7, then 30/60/90 — Most people decide within the first 90 days whether they stay; a short check-in in week one catches struggles early.
LEAD THE TEAM · GUIDE
Clear Roles & Expectations
End the Fog, End the Friction
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Unclear roles create most conflicts — People rarely fail on purpose; they fail on fog.
22Write simple role descriptions — Main responsibilities, decisions they own, who they report to — one page.
33Define what good looks like — Concrete standards (clean means…, safe means…, done means…) end debates.
44Clarify decision rights — What can they decide alone, what needs asking? Ambiguity here breeds frustration.
55Revisit when things change — New season, new project, new people — roles need maintenance.
66Address overlap openly — Where two people share ground, agree the split before it burns.
LEAD THE TEAM · GUIDE
Building Trust in the Team
The Foundation Everything Rests On
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Trust is the speed of the team — Everything is slower and heavier without it.
22Go first — Vulnerability from the leader (admitting limits, asking help) unlocks the team's honesty.
33Make speaking up safe — When mistakes and questions are welcomed, problems surface early; when they are punished, you simply stop hearing them.
44Keep confidences — One leaked secret costs a year of credibility.
55Be fair and consistent — Same rules for favorites and non-favorites — the team watches closely.
66Create real shared moments — Meals, celebrations and small adventures build what meetings can't.
77Repair quickly after breaches — Address broken trust directly; time alone heals nothing here.
LEAD THE TEAM · GUIDE
Celebrating Wins & Team Events
Joy That Builds Culture
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Celebration is fuel, not luxury — Teams that never celebrate learn that finishing doesn't matter.
22Mark milestones immediately — Small, quick recognition beats a big party six months later.
33Celebrate people, not only projects — Birthdays, anniversaries, personal wins — belonging grows here.
44Keep it inclusive and simple — Khmer and international staff, all budgets: shared food works everywhere.
55Use the ICF rhythms — All Hands, staff retreats and season closings are ready-made stages for honor.
66Tell the story — Photos and testimonies of what God did turn events into culture.

Manage the Work

MANAGE THE WORK · GUIDE
Planning & Setting Goals
From Vision to Weekly Steps
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Begin with the end — A clear picture of &quot;done and good&quot; is half the plan.
22Set few, clear goals — Three memorable goals beat ten forgotten ones; make them measurable.
33Break goals into weekly steps — Big goals move only when they appear in normal weeks.
44Plan with the team — People support what they helped create.
55Put it where everyone sees it — A visible plan (wall, board, doc) keeps priorities honest.
66Review rhythmically — Weekly small check, monthly deeper look, quarterly course correction.
MANAGE THE WORK · GUIDE
Problem Solving & Decision Making
Finding the Wise Way Forward
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Define the real problem first — An hour on the right question saves weeks on the wrong answer.
22Gather facts before opinions — Go see, ask those closest to the work, measure what you can.
33Generate options — always more than one — The first idea is rarely the best; force three alternatives.
44Decide at the right level — Involve those affected; escalate only what truly needs your leader.
55Decide, then commit fully — A good decision executed beats a perfect one delayed.
66Review what happened — Wins and failures both teach — if you debrief them.
MANAGE THE WORK · GUIDE
Reporting & Accountability
Truth That Builds Trust
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Reporting is serving, not surveillance — Your leader and ICF need truth to steer and to steward donors' trust.
22Know your numbers — Every leader tracks a handful of key figures for their area — know yours cold.
33Report rhythm and format — On time, in the agreed form, every time — reliability is the report.
44Bad news travels first — Problems reported early are projects; problems hidden become scandals.
55Hold your team accountable kindly — Follow up on agreements consistently — accountability is respect.
66Document decisions — Short written records prevent long painful debates later.
MANAGE THE WORK · GUIDE
Budget & Resources Basics
Faithful with Every Dollar
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Stewardship mindset — Every dollar was given in trust; leaders guard it like their own — and better.
22Know your budget — What you have, what's spent, what's left — check monthly, not yearly.
33Follow the purchase process — Requests, approvals and receipts: the process protects you.
44Care for materials &amp; assets — Maintenance, storage and inventory keep tools alive and costs down.
55Plan ahead for needs — Foreseeable expenses (repairs, seasons, events) belong in the plan, not in emergencies.
66Report transparently — Clean, honest financial reporting is a witness — inside and outside ICF.
MANAGE THE WORK · GUIDE
Policies, Safety & Admin Basics
Protecting People and Mission
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Rules serve the mission — Policies protect people, children and the ministry — lead them positively.
22Know the ones that matter — Child protection, safety, HR basics, leave and conduct: know where to find and how to apply them.
33Safety is leadership — You are responsible for safe work in your area: equipment, training, incidents.
44Child protection first — The protection of children is non-negotiable — know the code and enforce it.
55Handle HR admin on time — Contracts, leave, timesheets and APR deadlines respected — it's care, not paperwork.
66When unsure — ask — HR and Operations exist to help; guessing on policy questions creates damage.

Care & Culture

CARE & CULTURE · GUIDE
Pastoral Care for Your Team
Shepherding the People You Lead
1 DocumentDownload Document
11You lead people, not positions — Behind every role is a family, a story, a faith journey.
22Know your people's lives — Names of spouses and kids, home situations, current burdens — care starts with knowing.
33Recognize the signs of crisis — Withdrawal, performance drops and absence usually mean something at home.
44Be present in key moments — Weddings, funerals, sickness, births: showing up says more than programs.
55Care within your limits — You are a caring leader, not a counselor — connect people to pastoral or professional help.
66Pray for and with your team — The most underestimated leadership tool at ICF.
CARE & CULTURE · GUIDE
Leading Across Cultures
Building One Team from Two Worlds
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Different is not wrong — Time, directness, hierarchy and feedback work differently — learn before judging.
22Understand face and honor — Public correction shames; private, gentle words move mountains.
33Mind the language gap — Speak simply, check understanding, allow silence — a Khmer &quot;yes&quot; can mean &quot;I heard you&quot;, not &quot;I agree&quot;.
44Respect age within hierarchy — Younger leaders leading older staff is delicate; give elders honor and private explanations.
55Bridge both directions — Help internationals understand Khmer ways and Khmer staff navigate international expectations.
66Develop Khmer leadership deliberately — The future of ICF Cambodia is Khmer-led; make room, give voice, build confidence.
77Celebrate both cultures — Khmer New Year and Christmas, rice and Rösti — belonging needs both honored.
CARE & CULTURE · GUIDE
Faith & Devotions in the Team
Keeping the Mission at the Center
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Keep the why alive — ICF is ministry, not just an NGO; the work flows from the mission.
22Lead simple devotions — A verse, a thought, a prayer — 10 minutes that anchor the day.
33Let others lead too — Rotating devotions grows faith and confidence across the team.
44Connect faith and work — Show how cleaning, guarding and teaching serve God's story in Cambodia.
55Respect every journey — Not all staff are believers; invite warmly, never force.
66Pray through the hard seasons — Team crises are spiritual moments — lead them as such.
CARE & CULTURE · GUIDE
Wellbeing & Preventing Burnout
Sustainable People, Sustainable Ministry
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Burnout builds slowly — Months of small overload, not one hard week — watch trends, not days.
22Know the warning signs — Exhaustion, cynicism, mistakes, withdrawal — in yourself and your people.
33Fix the system, not just the person — Burnout comes mainly from unclear roles, friction and overload — change the conditions, not only the coping.
44Manage workload honestly — Chronic overtime is a planning problem, not a loyalty proof.
55Protect rest — Days off, holidays and Sabbath actually taken — leaders enforce rest by taking it.
66Talk about it openly — A team where tiredness can be said early rarely reaches breakdown.
77Act early and involve help — Reduce, redistribute, involve HR — a rested team member back beats a broken one gone.

Lead Leaders

LEAD LEADERS · GUIDE
From Team Leader to Leader of Leaders
Your Job Just Changed
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Your job changes fundamentally — From doing and directing to developing and aligning — let go of your old job.
22Lead through, not around — Never bypass your leaders to their teams; it destroys them in days.
33Multiply your standards — Your leaders must know your values and quality bar well enough to enforce them without you.
44Spend time where it multiplies — One hour developing a leader touches their whole team.
55Build the leader team — Your direct leaders must become a real team themselves, not rival chiefs.
66Keep one hand in reality — Stay close enough to the front line to smell problems early — without taking over.
LEAD LEADERS · GUIDE
Coaching a Leader Who Has Their Own Team
Coach the Leader, Not the Team
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Coach the leader, not their team — Resist solving their team's problems; grow their ability to solve them.
22Use their real cases — Coaching on today's actual conflict beats theory every time.
33Ask before you advise — &quot;What have you considered?&quot; first; your answer only after their thinking.
44Review their leading, not only their results — How they run meetings, develop people and handle conflict is the real KPI.
55Allow leadership styles to differ — Different from you can still be excellent; coach principles, not clones.
66Have the hard talks about leading — Standard-drops in their leadership need the same honest feedback as any performance issue.
LEAD LEADERS · GUIDE
Building Succession — Never Lead Alone
Stewardship Beyond Your Season
1 DocumentDownload Document
11Succession starts on day one — Leading without a successor plan is renting, not building.
22Build a bench, not just a №2 — Build several capable people per key area instead of betting on one successor.
33Share leadership visibly — Let others represent, decide and be seen — titles can wait, exposure can't.
44Document the essentials — Processes, contacts and passwords that live only in one head are risks.
55Plan transitions early — Handovers need months of overlap, not a week of notes.
66Measure yourself by what survives you — The test of your leadership is the team after you leave.
LEAD LEADERS · GUIDE
Aligning Multiple Teams
One Vision, One Voice
1 DocumentDownload Document
11One vision, many teams — Alignment means every team can say how their work serves the same ICF story.
22Fight silo thinking — &quot;My team first&quot; is natural and toxic; reward cross-team help publicly.
33Create shared rhythms — Joint leader meetings, aligned calendars and common goals hold teams together.
44Resolve inter-team conflicts fast — Leaders' conflicts cascade to whole departments within days.
55Balance resources fairly — Transparent, explained decisions on people and money prevent silent wars.
66Communicate as one leadership — Decide behind closed doors, then speak with one voice.
