ICF Leader's Guide

34 practical guidelines in 7 series — compiled by Matt · July 2026

Proverbs 27:17
1Growth is intentional — Nobody grows by accident; decide to grow before you decide what to do.
2Know yourself first — Use self-assessment (strengths, personality, feedback) to see your real starting point.
3Pick your growth areas — Choose max. two skills or character areas per season instead of improving everything at once.
4Build a simple plan — 30 minutes of deliberate growth daily beats a yearly seminar: reading, podcast, practice, reflection.
5Find growth partners — Share your plan with a mentor or peer who asks you about it regularly.
6Review every quarter — Look back, celebrate progress, adjust the plan — growth compounds when reviewed.

Proverbs 10:9
1Character is the foundation — People follow who you are before they follow what you say; trust is built or broken here.
2Do what you say — Keep small promises relentlessly; reliability in small things earns authority in big things.
3Be the same person everywhere — On campus, in the village, at church, online: one face, not two.
4Own your mistakes — Admitting errors quickly builds more trust than never making any.
5Handle money & resources cleanly — Transparency in finances and materials protects you and the ministry.
6Guard your private life — Integrity at home sustains integrity at work; hidden compromise always surfaces.

Psalm 90:12
1Not everything urgent is important — Learn to sort by impact, not by loudness.
2Plan the week, not just the day — 30 minutes on Monday planning saves hours of firefighting.
3The Jar of Life — Big rocks first: schedule your most important work before the small stuff fills the week.
4Protect focus time — Block 2 hours without Telegram for the work only you can do.
5Learn to say no well — Every yes is a no to something else; declining kindly is a leadership skill.
6Delegate before you drown — If your list never ends, the problem is usually keeping, not workload.

Mark 6:31
1You can't pour from an empty cup — Sustainable leadership starts with rest, sleep and Sabbath.
2Set working boundaries — Define your hours and response times, and let the team know them.
3Stay spiritually connected — Personal devotion is the engine, not the decoration, of ministry leadership.
4Watch your warning signs — Irritability, cynicism and exhaustion are data, not weakness.
5Have safe people — Every leader needs one place to be completely honest.
6Ask for help early — Talking to your leader or HR before breaking point is strength.

Philippians 2:4
1Why one-on-ones matter — 30 minutes of full attention builds more trust than a month of hallway talk.
2A steady rhythm — Fixed frequency (e.g., bi-weekly), fixed length, protected in the calendar — cancelling communicates priorities.
3Their agenda, not a status update — The one-on-one is for the person behind the work — their questions, blockers, growth and wellbeing.
4Four questions that always work — How are you? What's going well? Where are you stuck? How can I help?
5Listen more than you talk — They should talk 60–80% of the time; questions develop people, answers only inform them.
6Close with commitments — End with one or two concrete follow-ups and revisit them next time.

Exodus 18:21
1Why leaders don't delegate — Fear of quality, speed and losing control keeps you small; delegation is how both of you grow.
2Choose the right task & person — Match responsibility to their level: delegate outcomes to strong people, steps to learners.
3Hand over the why, not just the what — People own tasks when they understand purpose and boundaries.
4Agree on check-ins, don't hover — Set milestones together instead of micromanaging in between.
5Let them do it differently — 80% done their way beats 100% done your way — that's the price of growth.
6Stay accountable, then give credit — Delegating the task never delegates your responsibility: stay reachable, debrief, and give credit publicly.

Proverbs 20:5
1Coaching ≠ telling — A coach helps people find their own answer; advice creates dependence, questions create growth.
2The GROW path — Goal, Reality, Options, Will: four steps that structure any coaching conversation.
3Ask powerful questions — Open questions ("What have you tried?") unlock more than suggestions.
4Listen for the real issue — The presented problem is often not the actual problem; stay curious.
5Let them commit — A self-chosen next step has ten times the follow-through of an assigned one.
6Follow up faithfully — Coaching works through rhythm, not through one good conversation.

2 Timothy 2:2
1Look for faithfulness, not flash — Potential shows in reliability, hunger to learn and care for others.
2Give responsibility early — Small real ownership (an event, a shift, a project) reveals and builds leaders.
3The apprenticeship path — I do–you watch → we do together → you do–I support → you do–you teach.
4Develop in public — Let emerging leaders run meetings and make visible decisions while you back them.
5Talk about their future — Name what you see in them; many Khmer staff wait for permission to aspire.
6Release, don't hoard — Success is when your best person outgrows your team.

Ephesians 4:15
1Feedback is weekly, the review is a summary — The APR only collects what regular one-on-ones already made clear — nothing should surprise.
2Prepare with facts — Collect concrete examples for strengths and growth areas; vagueness helps no one.
3Start with self-assessment — Let them evaluate themselves first — most people are more honest than you expect.
4Balance honor and honesty — Real appreciation and clear growth points in the same conversation.
5Agree development goals — Two or three goals with support attached, written down together.
6Follow the ICF APR process — Use the official forms and timeline so HR and payroll stay aligned.

1 Thessalonians 5:11
1What gets recognized gets repeated — Praise is steering, not decoration.
2Be specific, timely and frequent — "Thank you for staying late to fix the pump on Tuesday" beats "good job"; recognized people are far more engaged.
3See the unseen roles — Practical teams (cleaning, security, kitchen, garden) feel overlooked most — your recognition matters double there.
4Know each person's fuel — Public praise, private words, responsibility, learning — motivation is individual.
5Celebrate progress, not only results — Effort and improvement deserve visibility, especially in hard seasons.
6Watch for demotivation signals — Silence, absence and minimum-effort are messages; address them early.
7Connect work to the vision — Every role serves the bigger ICF story — say it often.

1 Corinthians 14:40
1Every meeting needs a why — No purpose, no meeting; respect for time is respect for people.
2Information needs no meeting — Pure updates go out in writing; meet only to discuss, decide or connect.
3Prepare a simple agenda — Three points shared beforehand beat ninety unprepared minutes; attention drops after ~20 minutes.
4Start strong, start on time — An opener, a win or a devotion sets energy; starting late trains lateness.
5Involve everyone — Ask the quiet ones, park the long talkers kindly, use pairs for discussion.
6End with decisions & owners — Every point closes with what, who, until when — written down.
7Follow up visibly — Send the action list and start the next meeting by reviewing it.

Ephesians 4:29
1Feedback is care, not attack — Withholding honest feedback is withholding growth.
2Right time, right place — Praise publicly, correct privately, and never in anger.
3Describe, don't judge — Say what you observed and its effect, not what kind of person they are (SBI: Situation–Behavior–Impact).
4One thing at a time — A single clear point lands; a list of five overwhelms and hardens.
5Invite their view — Feedback is a dialogue: "How do you see it?" often changes the picture.
6End forward-looking — Agree the next step and express confidence — feedback should lift, not flatten.

James 1:19
1Your reaction sets the culture — Leaders who get defensive stop hearing truth forever.
2Listen fully before responding — Breathe, take notes, ask clarifying questions — don't build your defense while they talk.
3Thank first, evaluate later — Gratitude for courage, then 24 hours before deciding what to do with it.
4Look for the grain of truth — Even unfair feedback usually carries 10% you need.
5Ask for feedback actively — "What is one thing I could do better?" — regularly, and mean it.
6Close the loop — Tell people what you changed because of their feedback; nothing builds trust faster.

Ephesians 4:15
1Don't wait — the price rises — Avoided conversations become conflicts; address issues while they are small.
2Prepare heart and facts — Check your motive (help, not win) and gather concrete observations.
3Open honestly and kindly — Name the issue directly in the first two sentences; small talk fools no one.
4Listen for their story — There is always a perspective you don't have; curiosity de-escalates.
5Search for the way forward together — Move from who's right to what's next.
6Agree, document, follow up — Clear agreements, a short written note, and a check-in date.

Matthew 18:15
1Conflict is normal — escalation is optional — Healthy teams disagree; unhealthy teams go silent or explode.
2Go direct first — Encourage the two people to talk directly before you step in (Matthew 18 principle).
3Mediate fairly — Hear both sides separately, then together; no verdicts based on one story.
4Mind culture and face — In the Khmer context, saving face matters: private, calm, respectful settings work.
5Address gossip immediately — Gossip is conflict's fuel; redirect it to the person concerned every time.
6Rebuild the relationship — After resolution, create small cooperation moments so trust regrows.

Colossians 4:6
1Choose the right channel — Complex or emotional topics: face to face; logistics: Telegram; decisions: written.
2Respect response-time rules — Follow the ICF communication guidelines for availability and replies.
3Write clearly and kindly — Short sentences, clear ask, deadline; tone travels badly in text.
4Pass information down faithfully — What you hear in leaders' meetings must reach your team accurately and timely.
5Protect confidentiality — Know what is yours to share and what is not.
6Over-communicate change — People need to hear important news several times, in several ways.

Colossians 3:17
1Values are decisions, not posters — Culture is what you tolerate and what you celebrate.
2Model it first — Your team will copy your honesty, punctuality and respect — or your shortcuts.
3Tell value stories — Catch someone living a value and tell the story in the next meeting.
4Hire and onboard by values — Skills can be trained; values must be shared from day one.
5Confront value breaches — One ignored violation teaches everyone the values are optional.
6Review as a team — Once a season, ask honestly: which value are we living, which are we losing?

Romans 15:7
1The first week decides — People decide in days whether they belong; plan the first week like an event.
2Prepare before day one — Workplace, tools, schedule and a welcome — nothing says "unimportant" like an unprepared arrival.
3Assign a buddy — A peer for daily questions beats a leader for occasional ones.
4Explain the why of ICF — Vision, story and values before forms and procedures.
5Train in steps with check-ins — Small portions, early practice, weekly reviews in month one.
6Check in at day 7, then 30/60/90 — Most people decide within the first 90 days whether they stay; a short check-in in week one catches struggles early.

1 Corinthians 12:18
1Unclear roles create most conflicts — People rarely fail on purpose; they fail on fog.
2Write simple role descriptions — Main responsibilities, decisions they own, who they report to — one page.
3Define what good looks like — Concrete standards (clean means…, safe means…, done means…) end debates.
4Clarify decision rights — What can they decide alone, what needs asking? Ambiguity here breeds frustration.
5Revisit when things change — New season, new project, new people — roles need maintenance.
6Address overlap openly — Where two people share ground, agree the split before it burns.

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10
1Trust is the speed of the team — Everything is slower and heavier without it.
2Go first — Vulnerability from the leader (admitting limits, asking help) unlocks the team's honesty.
3Make speaking up safe — When mistakes and questions are welcomed, problems surface early; when they are punished, you simply stop hearing them.
4Keep confidences — One leaked secret costs a year of credibility.
5Be fair and consistent — Same rules for favorites and non-favorites — the team watches closely.
6Create real shared moments — Meals, celebrations and small adventures build what meetings can't.
7Repair quickly after breaches — Address broken trust directly; time alone heals nothing here.

Psalm 126:3
1Celebration is fuel, not luxury — Teams that never celebrate learn that finishing doesn't matter.
2Mark milestones immediately — Small, quick recognition beats a big party six months later.
3Celebrate people, not only projects — Birthdays, anniversaries, personal wins — belonging grows here.
4Keep it inclusive and simple — Khmer and international staff, all budgets: shared food works everywhere.
5Use the ICF rhythms — All Hands, staff retreats and season closings are ready-made stages for honor.
6Tell the story — Photos and testimonies of what God did turn events into culture.

Luke 14:28
1Begin with the end — A clear picture of "done and good" is half the plan.
2Set few, clear goals — Three memorable goals beat ten forgotten ones; make them measurable.
3Break goals into weekly steps — Big goals move only when they appear in normal weeks.
4Plan with the team — People support what they helped create.
5Put it where everyone sees it — A visible plan (wall, board, doc) keeps priorities honest.
6Review rhythmically — Weekly small check, monthly deeper look, quarterly course correction.

Proverbs 21:5
1Define the real problem first — An hour on the right question saves weeks on the wrong answer.
2Gather facts before opinions — Go see, ask those closest to the work, measure what you can.
3Generate options — always more than one — The first idea is rarely the best; force three alternatives.
4Decide at the right level — Involve those affected; escalate only what truly needs your leader.
5Decide, then commit fully — A good decision executed beats a perfect one delayed.
6Review what happened — Wins and failures both teach — if you debrief them.

1 Corinthians 4:2
1Reporting is serving, not surveillance — Your leader and ICF need truth to steer and to steward donors' trust.
2Know your numbers — Every leader tracks a handful of key figures for their area — know yours cold.
3Report rhythm and format — On time, in the agreed form, every time — reliability is the report.
4Bad news travels first — Problems reported early are projects; problems hidden become scandals.
5Hold your team accountable kindly — Follow up on agreements consistently — accountability is respect.
6Document decisions — Short written records prevent long painful debates later.

Luke 16:10
1Stewardship mindset — Every dollar was given in trust; leaders guard it like their own — and better.
2Know your budget — What you have, what's spent, what's left — check monthly, not yearly.
3Follow the purchase process — Requests, approvals and receipts: the process protects you.
4Care for materials & assets — Maintenance, storage and inventory keep tools alive and costs down.
5Plan ahead for needs — Foreseeable expenses (repairs, seasons, events) belong in the plan, not in emergencies.
6Report transparently — Clean, honest financial reporting is a witness — inside and outside ICF.

Colossians 3:23
1Rules serve the mission — Policies protect people, children and the ministry — lead them positively.
2Know the ones that matter — Child protection, safety, HR basics, leave and conduct: know where to find and how to apply them.
3Safety is leadership — You are responsible for safe work in your area: equipment, training, incidents.
4Child protection first — The protection of children is non-negotiable — know the code and enforce it.
5Handle HR admin on time — Contracts, leave, timesheets and APR deadlines respected — it's care, not paperwork.
6When unsure — ask — HR and Operations exist to help; guessing on policy questions creates damage.

Galatians 6:2
1You lead people, not positions — Behind every role is a family, a story, a faith journey.
2Know your people's lives — Names of spouses and kids, home situations, current burdens — care starts with knowing.
3Recognize the signs of crisis — Withdrawal, performance drops and absence usually mean something at home.
4Be present in key moments — Weddings, funerals, sickness, births: showing up says more than programs.
5Care within your limits — You are a caring leader, not a counselor — connect people to pastoral or professional help.
6Pray for and with your team — The most underestimated leadership tool at ICF.

Revelation 7:9
1Different is not wrong — Time, directness, hierarchy and feedback work differently — learn before judging.
2Understand face and honor — Public correction shames; private, gentle words move mountains.
3Mind the language gap — Speak simply, check understanding, allow silence — a Khmer "yes" can mean "I heard you", not "I agree".
4Respect age within hierarchy — Younger leaders leading older staff is delicate; give elders honor and private explanations.
5Bridge both directions — Help internationals understand Khmer ways and Khmer staff navigate international expectations.
6Develop Khmer leadership deliberately — The future of ICF Cambodia is Khmer-led; make room, give voice, build confidence.
7Celebrate both cultures — Khmer New Year and Christmas, rice and Rösti — belonging needs both honored.

Colossians 3:23
1Keep the why alive — ICF is ministry, not just an NGO; the work flows from the mission.
2Lead simple devotions — A verse, a thought, a prayer — 10 minutes that anchor the day.
3Let others lead too — Rotating devotions grows faith and confidence across the team.
4Connect faith and work — Show how cleaning, guarding and teaching serve God's story in Cambodia.
5Respect every journey — Not all staff are believers; invite warmly, never force.
6Pray through the hard seasons — Team crises are spiritual moments — lead them as such.

Mark 6:31
1Burnout builds slowly — Months of small overload, not one hard week — watch trends, not days.
2Know the warning signs — Exhaustion, cynicism, mistakes, withdrawal — in yourself and your people.
3Fix the system, not just the person — Burnout comes mainly from unclear roles, friction and overload — change the conditions, not only the coping.
4Manage workload honestly — Chronic overtime is a planning problem, not a loyalty proof.
5Protect rest — Days off, holidays and Sabbath actually taken — leaders enforce rest by taking it.
6Talk about it openly — A team where tiredness can be said early rarely reaches breakdown.
7Act early and involve help — Reduce, redistribute, involve HR — a rested team member back beats a broken one gone.

Exodus 18:21
1Your job changes fundamentally — From doing and directing to developing and aligning — let go of your old job.
2Lead through, not around — Never bypass your leaders to their teams; it destroys them in days.
3Multiply your standards — Your leaders must know your values and quality bar well enough to enforce them without you.
4Spend time where it multiplies — One hour developing a leader touches their whole team.
5Build the leader team — Your direct leaders must become a real team themselves, not rival chiefs.
6Keep one hand in reality — Stay close enough to the front line to smell problems early — without taking over.

Proverbs 20:5
1Coach the leader, not their team — Resist solving their team's problems; grow their ability to solve them.
2Use their real cases — Coaching on today's actual conflict beats theory every time.
3Ask before you advise — "What have you considered?" first; your answer only after their thinking.
4Review their leading, not only their results — How they run meetings, develop people and handle conflict is the real KPI.
5Allow leadership styles to differ — Different from you can still be excellent; coach principles, not clones.
6Have the hard talks about leading — Standard-drops in their leadership need the same honest feedback as any performance issue.

2 Timothy 2:2
1Succession starts on day one — Leading without a successor plan is renting, not building.
2Build a bench, not just a №2 — Build several capable people per key area instead of betting on one successor.
3Share leadership visibly — Let others represent, decide and be seen — titles can wait, exposure can't.
4Document the essentials — Processes, contacts and passwords that live only in one head are risks.
5Plan transitions early — Handovers need months of overlap, not a week of notes.
6Measure yourself by what survives you — The test of your leadership is the team after you leave.

Ephesians 4:11–12
1One vision, many teams — Alignment means every team can say how their work serves the same ICF story.
2Fight silo thinking — "My team first" is natural and toxic; reward cross-team help publicly.
3Create shared rhythms — Joint leader meetings, aligned calendars and common goals hold teams together.
4Resolve inter-team conflicts fast — Leaders' conflicts cascade to whole departments within days.
5Balance resources fairly — Transparent, explained decisions on people and money prevent silent wars.
6Communicate as one leadership — Decide behind closed doors, then speak with one voice.
